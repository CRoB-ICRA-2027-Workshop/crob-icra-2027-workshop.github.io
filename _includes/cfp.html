Call for Papers

We invite 2–4 page extended abstracts on new, preliminary, and in-progress work in robot co-design. Each submission should be framed as addressing one of the three workshop questions in the About section. Position pieces, benchmark proposals, and negative results are explicitly in scope, as are submissions from industrial labs and hardware-focused teams. Topics include, but are not limited to:

  • Evidence for choosing a specialist body over adapting a generalist policy, or the reverse
  • Co-design with policies pretrained across many embodiments
  • Morphology optimization and generative design of robot bodies
  • Validating learned design evaluators under controller retraining and sim-to-real transfer
  • Fabrication and hardware tests of optimized designs
  • Benchmarks and reporting standards for body-selection claims

A program committee drawn from robot learning, co-design, and hardware design gives each abstract at least two single-blind reviews, and reviewers with a conflict of interest recuse themselves. We will accept up to 20 posters, and up to ten selected abstracts will give two-minute pitches. A portion of pitch slots is reserved for student-led and early-career work. Accepted abstracts will be hosted on this website.
